Which standard is which

Two ASTM specifications from the early 1990s define nearly every serial interface between a laboratory instrument and a laboratory information system (LIS). ASTM handed them to NCCLS, now CLSI, which publishes them as the LIS series; ASTM withdrew its own editions. The content carried over; CLSI revised the text editorially.

TopicASTM (withdrawn)CLSI (current)Covers
Low-level protocolE1381-91, E1381-95LIS1-A, now LIS01-A2 (2008) Serial line, link establishment, frames, checksum, retries, timeouts
Message contentE1394-91, E1394-97LIS2-A, now LIS02-A2 (2004) Records H P O R C Q L M S, fields, delimiters, codes
Clinical observations between computer systemsE1238LIS5-A System-to-system result reporting; the ancestor of the HL7 v2 observation segments (OBR, OBX)

Names in use for the same thing: "ASTM protocol", "ASTM 1394", "E1394", "LIS2", "LIS02-A2", "CLSI LIS02". In instrument manuals "ASTM" without a number nearly always means the pair E1381 + E1394. CLSI moved to two-digit numbers (LIS1 → LIS01), so both spellings exist.

Differences between the editions matter little in practice: record types, field positions and codes are the same in E1394-97 and LIS02-A2, and the link rules of E1381-95 and LIS01-A2 match. What differs between interfaces is the version string an instrument puts in H-13 (see the field reference) and how strictly it follows the rules, not the edition it cites.

The two layers are independent: a LIS02 message can travel over another link (a file, a TCP stream without framing, an HL7 envelope), and LIS01 framing can carry other content. lisbench keeps them apart the same way: frame/deframe and the conform suite work on LIS01, decode, validate and the sessions on LIS02.

LIS01-A2: the link

Physical layer

A point-to-point serial line. Default 9600 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit; other rates and settings by agreement of both sides. No hardware or software flow control is needed, because the link protocol itself paces the data (one frame, then wait for the answer).

Phases

  1. Establishment. The side with data sends ENQ. The receiver answers ACK (ready) or NAK (busy).
  2. Transfer. The sender sends frames one at a time; each is answered with ACK (accepted, next frame), NAK (rejected, send it again) or EOT (receiver interrupt, see below).
  3. Termination. The sender sends EOT; both sides are idle again and either may start.

Frame

<STX> FN text <ETB or ETX> C1 C2 <CR> <LF>
  • FN: frame number, one ASCII digit 0–7. The first frame of a transfer is 1, then counting on modulo 8 (… 7, 0, 1 …) across all frames of the transfer.
  • text: at most 240 characters; a frame is at most 247 characters in total. A record ends with CR inside the text.
  • ETX ends the last frame of a record, ETB an intermediate frame of a record longer than 240 characters.
  • C1 C2: checksum, the sum of all characters from FN through ETB/ETX inclusive, modulo 256, written as two upper-case hex digits.
  • Restricted characters must not appear in the text: SOH, STX, ETX, EOT, ENQ, ACK, DLE, DC1–DC4, NAK, SYN, ETB and LF. CR only ends a record.

Example: the frame text 1L|1|N<CR><ETX> sums to 0x1D3 → checksum D3. Try lisbench frame to see it for any record.

LIS01: conventions and known issues

  • Over TCP/IP the data link layer is usually kept unchanged (ENQ/ACK, frames, EOT) on a plain socket. Which side listens differs per instrument and must be configured. Some instruments drop the link layer on TCP and send bare records ending with CR, or wrap the message in an HL7-style MLLP envelope.
  • One record per frame, split with ETB only when longer than 240 characters. Some senders pack several records into one frame; a robust receiver accepts both.
  • Checksum in lower-case hex, or computed without FN, or including STX.
  • Frame numbers that start at 0, or restart at 1 for every record.
  • LF missing after CR, or a frame longer than 247 characters (the whole message in one frame). a profile can raise the accepted text length.
  • Receiver interrupt not honoured, or not sent at all (the receiver lets a long transfer run). profile field honours_receiver_interrupt.
  • Contention handled the other way round: the instrument yields to the LIS. If both sides yield, or both insist, the link deadlocks until a timeout. profile field contention_winner.
  • A LIS that needs longer than 15 s to answer a query: the instrument has already given up. Query answers have to come from a fast lookup, not from a slow report.
  • An instrument that resets when the serial port is opened (common with USB adapters and microcontroller boards), and sends nothing for some seconds.
  • A port opened while the partner is in the middle of a transfer: the rest of its frame arrives first. Outside a transfer only ENQ starts something, so a receiver drops everything before the next ENQ; STX is not a point to synchronise on, a frame is valid only after ENQ and ACK. bytes before the first ENQ after opening are dropped and noted once; later bytes outside a transfer give one warning per burst.

LIS02-A2: the message

Structure

A message starts with an H (header) record and ends with an L (terminator) record. In between the records form a hierarchy given only by their order:

H                         header: delimiters, sender, receiver, version, time
  P                       patient (may be nearly empty, e.g. for QC)
    O                     order: one specimen and one or more tests
      R                   result for one test of that order
        C                 comment on the record just before it (any level)
      R ...
    O ...
  P ...
L                         terminator
---
H  Q  L                   a query (request for information) message
M                         manufacturer-defined record, anywhere the maker says
S                         scientific record (reserved; hardly ever seen)
  • Sequence numbers (field 2) start at 1 and count the records of one type under the same parent: P 1, 2, … per message; O starts again at 1 under each P, R again under each O. lisbench validate checks order, hierarchy and numbering.
  • Field numbering: field 1 is the record type letter, so O-3 is the third field of an O record. lisbench numbers fields this way everywhere.
  • Delimiters are declared in the header: the character right after H is the field delimiter, the next three (H-2) are the repeat, component and escape delimiters. The usual set is | \ ^ &, written H|\^&.
  • Escapes inside field values, shown with & as escape delimiter: &F& field, &S& component, &R& repeat, &E& escape delimiter, &X..& hex bytes, &Z..& local (left to the partners).
  • Empty fields are written as nothing between delimiters; trailing empty fields may be left out entirely.
  • Date and time: YYYYMMDDHHMMSS, shortened from the right when less is known (a birthdate is YYYYMMDD). No time zone.
  • Character set: 7-bit ASCII; anything else only by agreement. decoder options --charset, --local-escape, and profiles.

LIS02: conventions and known issues

  • Test codes go into the fourth component of the universal test ID: ^^^GLU. The first three components (a "universal" code, name, type) are mostly left empty.
  • Several tests in one O record are separated by the repeat delimiter (^^^GLU\^^^K); others send one O record per test. A LIS must read both.
  • H-5 sender name as name^version^serial, which identifies the instrument and its software release.
  • Delimiters hard-coded instead of read from H; free text (comments, names) with an unescaped | or ^, which shifts all following fields.
  • Sequence numbers that never restart, or count records of all types together.
  • 8-bit text (Latin-1, a code page, or UTF-8) without saying so, especially in patient names.
  • Times with separators (2026-10-26 09:42) or without seconds; times in UTC on one side and local time on the other.
  • Vendor manuals that number fields differently: from 0, or with the field delimiter in H counted as a field. Check one example message against the manual's numbering before mapping fields.
  • Values that are not numbers in a numeric test: <0.5, >500, ***, an error text. Comparisons and statistics must handle them. watches compare with < or > only against numeric values.

Workflows

ModeMessagesNotes
Unidirectional / unsolicitedinstrument → LIS: H P O R L Tests are chosen at the instrument; the LIS matches results to its own orders by specimen ID and test, or stores them without an order. LIS session setting "results without an order": accept, keep for review, ignore.
Batch (download)LIS → instrument: H P O L (O-26 = O, O-12 = N); later instrument → LIS: R The LIS sends orders ahead; a cancellation is an O record with action code C.
Host query (query mode)instrument: H Q L; LIS: H P O L (O-26 = Q, L-3 = F) or "no order"; instrument: H P O R L The instrument reads a barcode and asks. "No order" is answered with O-26 = Y (no order for this test/specimen), Z (no record of this patient), or a message with only H and L (L-3 = I). Which one an instrument expects varies. LIS session setting "answer for a specimen without order".

Host query is often called "bidirectional" or "query mode" in manuals; batch is called "download" or "broadcast". Results going to the LIS are sometimes called "upload".

IHE LAW (Laboratory Analytical Workflow) is an HL7 v2.5.1 profile designed for the instrument-to-LIS interface, as a successor to the ASTM pair; CLSI took it up as AUTO16. HL7 v2's observation segments share their ancestry with ASTM E1238, which is why OBR/OBX and O/R look alike.
